Brand Book / Toolkit
tkxel.com

Consistency, recognizability and resonance carry our visual story. Our aesthetic is technical and precise structured architecture, bold elements balanced against clean and minimal composition, generous negative space that lets the message hold the room.

The system reflects the work: applied AI, delivered by engineers who have shipped it. Deep expertise, an optimistic outlook, and a problem-solving instinct pointed at our clients' hardest questions.

The tkxel mark is the centerpiece. Around it sits a modular system, a defined set of components, colors and layouts that combine in an effectively unlimited number of ways, without ever producing something that looks unlike us.

2.1

Shape System

Nine shapes · One shape per document

Every long-form cover carries one shape from the tkxel system. The shape is not decorative and is not chosen by preference — it is determined by the document's primary subject. Select a shape to see the cover it produces.

Why These Shapes

Each shape in the tkxel system is named for its visual character rather than its current application — Orbit, Spark, Cluster — not for the service it happens to represent today. Names are short, expressive and abstract, which allows an aspect to be used across services, industries and future offerings without becoming literal or constrained.

Shape Names

Reference · All nine shapes

Each shape within the tkxel system is intentionally named to reflect both its visual character and its conceptual role.

Names are short, expressive, and abstract, allowing shapes to be used across services, industries, and future offerings without becoming literal or restrictive. This naming approach supports longevity and flexibility, ensuring the system can evolve as tkxel grows.

Shape names act as a shared language between designers, marketers, and product teams, making the system easier to understand, reference, and apply consistently.

Rules

Five governing constraints
  • One per documentNo combining, no alternating between chapters. If a topic genuinely needs two shapes, it is two documents.
  • PlacementFixed anchor across all documents in a format. Consistency of position is what makes the set read as a series.
  • RecurrenceCover and chapter openers use the same shape. The shape identifies the document, not the chapter.
  • No new shapesIf nothing fits, the subject sits outside tkxel's defined domains — a positioning question, not a design one.
  • Refer by nameAlways "Spark", never "the star one". The name is the reference in briefs, file names and layer names.

2.6

Light & Dark

Same geometry, two grounds

Shapes are built once and rendered in two modes. The geometry never changes — only the gradient. Toggle the mode on the Cover Generator slide to see both.

ModeGroundShape gradientUse
LightWhiteMid to deep blueBody pages, checklists, print, anything text-dense
DarkDeep blue or near-blackPale to mid blueCovers, section openers, social, statement slides
  • Never invertA light-mode gradient placed on a dark ground loses its edge and reads as a smudge. Use the mode's own gradient.
  • Contrast floorThe shape is a graphic element, not text — but any type sitting over it still meets 4.5:1. Keep type off the densest part of the gradient.
  • Blue-on-bluePermitted on dark blue grounds, where the shape sits as a tonal watermark. Keep opacity low enough that overlaid type stays legible.
  • One mode per surfaceA single page never mixes light and dark shape treatments.

0.2

Using the Shapes

Placement · Scale · Cropping · Misuse

The shapes carry more identity weight than any other element in the collateral system. Applied consistently they make nine formats read as one family; applied loosely they become generic gradient decoration. These rules govern every appearance.

Shape and mode

Two separate decisions, and only one of them is strategic.

ShapeMode
DecidesWhat the document is aboutHow the cover looks
Chosen bySubject — see Selecting a shapeDesigner's judgement
ConstrainedYes. One correct answer per document.No. Either mode is always valid.
Two white papers on the same subject carry the same shape. Mode is what tells them apart on a shelf or a landing page. Nothing more is being signalled — a dark cover does not mean the document is more important, more technical, or newer.
  • Cover and openers onlyMode governs covers, chapter openers and back covers. Body pages are always light — reversed type is unreadable at length.
  • One mode per documentA document does not switch mode partway through its openers.
  • Adjacent covers differWhere two documents in the same domain will be seen together — a resource page, a carousel, a sales folder — give them different modes.
  • Contrast still appliesMode is a visual choice, not an exemption. All type meets 4.5:1 in either mode.

The pattern above is deliberate: shapes appear on openers and covers only. They mark thresholds — the start of a document, a chapter, a section. A shape on a body page or a content slide competes with the content it should be introducing.

Placement

  • Fixed anchorWithin a format, the shape occupies the same position on every cover. Top-right for portrait documents, upper-right quadrant for 16:9. Consistency of position is what makes a set read as a series.
  • Crop at two edgesCover shapes are scaled up and bled off the page, never floated fully contained. At cover scale the shape reads as an atmospheric field rather than a recognisable mark — that is correct.
  • Never centeredA centered shape reads as a logo. These are not logos.
  • Behind type, not under itThe shape sits in the negative space the title leaves. If the title has to move to accommodate the shape, the shape is too large.
  • One per surfaceNever two shapes on the same page, even at different scales.

Scale & proportion

  • Minimum size24px. Below that the gradient blur collapses into a flat blob and the geometry stops reading.
  • Blur scales with the shapeThe softness is proportional. Scaling a shape up without scaling its blur produces a hard-edged version that doesn't match the system.
  • Never distortScale proportionally only. No stretching, squashing or skewing.
  • RotationFree at cover scale, where the shape is atmospheric. At opener scale and below, where the shape stays recognisable, rotate only Spark, Bolt and Arc — never Orbit, Cluster, Block, Flow, Twin Flow or Pulse.

Clear space

Minimum clear space on all sides equals 25% of the shape's width. Nothing enters that field — no type, no rules, no logos, no other graphic elements. Where a shape crops off the page edge, clear space applies only to the visible sides.

Color

  • Brand blue onlyShapes render in the blue gradient, in the mode-appropriate direction. No other hue.
  • No flat fillsThe gradient and its blur are the shape's character. A flat blue version is a different mark.
  • No outlinesShapes are solid forms. No stroked, hollow or line-art variants.
  • Tonal useOn dark blue grounds a shape may sit as a low-contrast watermark. This is the only permitted opacity reduction.

Misuse

Do

  • Crop the shape off the page edge on covers
  • Keep the same shape across a document's cover and chapter openers
  • Scale the blur proportionally with the shape
  • Hold the anchor position across every document in a format
  • Refer to shapes by name in briefs and layer names

Don't

  • Don't place two shapes on one surface
  • Don't recolor, outline or flatten the gradient
  • Don't stretch, skew or distort
  • Don't set type over the densest part of the gradient
  • Don't use a shape as a bullet, icon or divider
  • Don't create new shapes or modify existing geometry
  • Don't place shapes on body pages or content slides

Shapes are not icons

The most common failure mode is treating shapes as an icon set — using Bolt beside a "fast delivery" bullet, or Block next to a security feature. Shapes identify a document, not a concept within it. Iconography is a separate system with its own stroke weight and grid; use that instead.

Selecting a shape

  • Match the argumentChoose for the document's primary subject, not its secondary themes. A paper on securing AI workloads is Block if the argument is protection, Spark if it is capability.
  • Ask what the reader takes awayThe takeaway determines the shape, not the topic keywords in the title.
  • Same subject, same shapeTwo documents on the same subject carry the same shape. Differentiate them with mode, not by reaching for a second shape.
  • When nothing fitsThe subject sits outside tkxel's defined domains. That is a positioning question to resolve before design — not a reason to draw a tenth shape.
